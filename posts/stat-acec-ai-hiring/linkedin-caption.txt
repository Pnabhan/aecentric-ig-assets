More than half of engineering firms are now investing in dedicated AI focused talent. That comes from ACEC's Q1 2026 Engineering Business Sentiment survey, 628 member firm executives, fielded in January.

Hiring is a more honest adoption signal than a pilot count. A pilot costs a few licenses and somebody's spare afternoon. A role costs a salary line, a reporting structure, and a hiring committee's attention, and firms do not create one for an experiment they expect to abandon by Q3.

It also tells you where this is heading inside firms like yours. When AI capability becomes somebody's actual job rather than an initiative bolted onto a project manager's week, it stops being a technology question and becomes an operating one.

If nobody at your firm owns this yet, that is the gap worth closing first.
