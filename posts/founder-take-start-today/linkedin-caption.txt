We hear a version of the same hesitation from a lot of firm leaders: they're waiting for the right AI tool, the right pilot, the right moment, before they start. It's an understandable instinct, but it isn't a neutral one. Waiting has a cost, it just doesn't show up anywhere you'd normally look for it.

The firms pulling ahead didn't wait for a clean setup. They picked one workflow, started with something imperfect, fixed what broke along the way, and kept going. A year later, that head start compounds into a real gap between them and the firms still evaluating.

Hesitation feels careful. It feels like the responsible choice. It isn't free, and a year or two from now the difference between the firms that started and the firms that waited will be obvious to everyone, including the ones that waited.

Our advice stays the same as always: pick one real workflow, run it as a pilot with a defined scope and a KPI, and start. You can always refine it. You can't refine a decision you haven't made yet.
