In data center development, power is the real constraint, not land or capital.

We see developers evaluate a site the way they always have: proximity to a market, cost of land, ease of entitlement. None of that matters much if the utility cannot deliver power on a useful timeline. Capital and land follow power, not the other way around.

That reshapes which markets win. A secondary market that can energize a site sooner is a better bet than a primary market stuck in a multi year interconnection queue. The developers getting this right are starting to think and act like energy companies, not like traditional real estate developers: they underwrite substation capacity and utility timelines as carefully as they underwrite the building itself.
