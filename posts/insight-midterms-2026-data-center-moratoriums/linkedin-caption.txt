Data centers were on the ballot on November 3, and they lost almost everywhere.

Voters sided against data centers in 40 of 41 local ballot measures across 10 states, 21 of them in Ohio, according to Ballotpedia's count reported by CNBC. The one exception was Harwood, North Dakota. In CNBC's voter poll, 56 percent of voters called data centers a crucial or major factor in their vote.

Three governors-elect campaigned on pausing new projects. In Texas, Gina Hinojosa promised a moratorium on data center approvals until lawmakers pass new rules; the Legislature meets in January and she takes office January 19. Governor Abbott had already paused approvals in August pending audits. In Ohio, Amy Acton called for a conditional moratorium unless developers cover all utility costs, use union labor and meet environmental standards. In Georgia, Keisha Lance Bottoms promised a temporary statewide pause to assess the effect on power bills, communities and the environment.

Sources: CNBC, Texas Tribune and Fortune, all November 4, 2026. Results are unofficial until certified, and none of these promises is law yet.

What we take from it: this reads less like a ban than new terms. Pay for your own power, protect the water, show the community the cost. For AEC firms working on data centers in these states, utility cost, water and community impact studies move from the end of the schedule to the front. Plan for January now.
