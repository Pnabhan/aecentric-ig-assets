Three states have now acted on data center approvals in the span of about ten weeks, and each used a different lever.

Texas: on September 21, Governor Abbott directed the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality to halt permit issuance for data center projects until ERCOT completes a statewide grid and water audit, expected in mid-December.

New York: Executive Order 62, signed July 14, imposed a one year moratorium on discretionary permits for data centers drawing 50 megawatts or more, making it the first state to freeze new large load data center construction statewide.

New Hampshire: on September 18, Senate President Sharon Carson said the chamber would pursue its own moratorium legislation, a policy shift after two new data center proposals surfaced in the Concord area.

Three different mechanisms, governor's order, executive order, legislative signal, pointing at the same underlying cause: grid capacity, water use, and ratepayer cost have outrun the approval process in these states.

For firms running data center work, this is not a policy story to read past. It is a scheduling and scoping problem. A project in predesign in a state that pauses tomorrow sits exactly where it was. A project mid permitting sits somewhere worse.

We're curious how other firms are underwriting that risk into their pipelines right now. If your state paused new approvals tomorrow, what actually breaks first: the schedule, the financing, or the client conversation?
