Most resistance to AI on a project team isn't about quality.

It's about identity. The work feels like the craft, and telling someone that a tool can handle part of it can land as a judgment on their skill, even when that isn't the intent. Pushing back on the resistance directly rarely works, because it isn't really a disagreement about the tool's output. It's about what the work means to the person doing it.

We think that reaction deserves respect, not an argument. The more effective move is showing someone the hours a workflow change actually gives back to them, concretely, on their own projects, and then letting them decide what to do with that time. People adopt tools faster when they get to choose, and slower when they feel like the choice was already made for them.
