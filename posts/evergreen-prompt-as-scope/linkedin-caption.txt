We tell AEC professionals prompting is a new skill they need to learn. It isn't. It's a skill most of them already have, under a different name.

A good scope of work names the deliverable, states the standard it has to meet, lists the constraints, and says what's explicitly excluded. A good prompt does exactly the same four things, just aimed at an AI tool instead of a project team.

Tell the tool precisely what you want back, not just the general topic. Give it the standard or template to follow, the way you'd cite a code edition or a spec section in a scope. And state what's out of bounds, so it doesn't quietly wander into work nobody asked for.

We've found this framing changes how prompting lands with senior staff in particular. They don't think of themselves as needing to pick up a novelty tech skill. They think of themselves as already being expert scope writers, and they are, which makes the transfer feel a lot closer to home.
