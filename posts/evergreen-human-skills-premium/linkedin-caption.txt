AI tools are getting fast at the first-pass work in AEC: drafting, redlines, submittal sorting. They are not getting any better at knowing which of those things actually matters on a given project. That's still on the people running it.

We'd argue that's where the real AI premium sits right now, and it isn't a technical one. It's not about who can operate the tool best. It's about who still has to decide what the output should say before it reaches a client or goes out under a stamp.

As AI absorbs more of the first-pass production work, the judgment layered on top of it doesn't shrink. It becomes more visible, and it becomes the harder, higher-value part of the job, not the part that's easiest to replace.

Our advice to firms: treat that judgment as a skill worth building on purpose. Real projects, real review, real feedback, the same way you'd develop any other core competency. Don't assume it just develops on its own alongside the tools.
