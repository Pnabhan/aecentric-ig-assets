The Federal Reserve's November Beige Book quoted a North Carolina builder who attributed wage pressure partly to trades workers traveling out of state to work on data center projects for higher wages.

It is a single report from the Richmond District, not a survey result. But it describes a pull many AEC firms are feeling. Data center programs pay well, move fast, and recruit from the same pool of field staff, inspectors and project engineers.

Matching every offer on pay is hard. What firms can control is the daily experience of the work: less time on repetitive documentation, cleaner handoffs, fewer late nights fixing coordination errors. That is where well-chosen AI tools can make a job easier to stay in.
