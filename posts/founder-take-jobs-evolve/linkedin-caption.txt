We hear this concern in almost every AI conversation we have with AEC leadership teams: what happens to our people when the tool gets good enough.

History gives a useful, if imperfect, guide. A century ago, most of the workforce was in farming. Mechanization moved that work, and new jobs replaced it, jobs nobody in 1926 could have described. The shift wasn't painless and it wasn't instant, but the pattern held.

We think AI is the next version of that shift inside AEC, not the end of the profession. The work changes. Judgment, coordination, and review become more valuable, not less. The firms that plan for that shift now, deliberately, are the ones whose people come out ahead of it.
