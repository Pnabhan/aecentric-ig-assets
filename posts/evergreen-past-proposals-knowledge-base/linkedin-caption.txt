Most firms have ten years of proposals sitting in a folder tree that nobody can actually search. That collection is a knowledge base already. It has just never been treated like one.

The proposals that won contain language worth reusing: project descriptions, staff resumes, and approach narratives that a reviewer already approved. When we help a firm prepare for AI-assisted proposal writing, one of the first steps is curating those sections into a single, current library, not everything ever written, just the pieces that worked.

Skip that step and an AI tool pulls from whatever it can find: old boilerplate, inconsistent formatting, language from a proposal that lost. The library is the real work here. Once it exists, drafting the next proposal gets meaningfully easier, and the model has something legitimate to work from instead of inventing language that reads generic.
