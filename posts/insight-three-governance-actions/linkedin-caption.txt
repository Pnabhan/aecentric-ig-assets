Most firms we talk to want an AI policy and are not sure what goes in one. Three actions cover most of the risk.

First, map what your firm is actually using. That includes the tools people adopted on their own, which is usually a longer list than leadership expects. You cannot govern a subscription you do not know exists.

Second, classify risk by where the output lands. Anything that reaches a client deliverable is high risk by definition, and a licensed professional has to own it. Internal research and a first draft of a memo are not the same category.

Third, write the acceptable use policy down. Data handling, output review, sign off. Three sections, plain language, short enough that people read it instead of filing it.

The rule underneath all three is the one worth repeating to your team. AI can assist, but a licensed professional must review, validate, and take responsibility for the work. Everything else is implementation detail.
